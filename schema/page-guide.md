# Page guide

> Generated by `scripts/build.py` from the comments in the templates in `schema/`. Do not edit by hand; edit the template comments instead.

Each page type answers a fixed set of questions. This page lists them, so readers who do not open the raw template files can see what each section is for.

## Metric pages

Source: [`schema/metric-template.md`](metric-template.md)

### Front matter fields

- `id`: lowercase-hyphenated; must equal the file name without .md
- `fields`: e.g. hci, information-retrieval, recommender-systems, visualization, human-ai-interaction, llm-evaluation
- `status`: see schema/vocabulary.yaml#status
- `last_reviewed`: YYYY-MM-DD
- `construct`
  - `statement`: one sentence in plain language: the "spirit" of what this number measures, across all its uses. Authoritative.
  - `measures`: construct ids from constructs/ (one or more); the place(s) this metric sits in the construct DAG
  - `subject`: vocabulary: subject
  - `aspect`: vocabulary: aspect
  - `perspective`: vocabulary: perspective
  - `quantity_form`: vocabulary: quantity_form
  - `context`: vocabulary: context
- `operationalization`
  - `measure_type`: direct | proxy | mixed, relative to construct.statement
  - `research_method`: vocabulary: research_method (how the data is gathered)
  - `route`: strategy ids from strategies/ (how the data becomes a number)
  - `collection_mode`: vocabulary: collection_mode
  - `data_source`: vocabulary: data_source
  - `better`: higher | lower | target-range | context-dependent
  - `validity_concern`: how much thought the gap between number and construct deserves
    - `level`: vocabulary: validity_concern
    - `note`: one line: why
- `cost`
  - `level`: vocabulary: cost_level
  - `requires`: free text, e.g. "participants", "relevance judgments", "eye tracker"
- `related`: each item: {metric: <id>, kind: operational | conceptual | empirical, note: "..."}
- `objectives`
  - `sustained-use`: direction: positive | negative | non-monotonic | unknown
- `sources`: keys in references/bibliography.yaml

### Sections

- **1. What is measured**: What property, of what subject, in what context, does this number capture?
  - **1.1 Definition**: Plain-language definition. Formula goes in 2.4.
  - **1.2 Construct: property of subject in context**: Expand construct.statement: what property, of what subject, in what context. Write it at the level of the "spirit" of the measurement, so it holds for every use in 3.1. The links to the construct DAG (front matter construct.measures) are listed automatically below; do not edit between the markers.
  - **1.3 What it does not measure**: Common misreadings; neighbouring constructs this metric is often confused with.
- **2. How it is measured**: How are observations gathered and turned into this number, and how far can the number be trusted?
  - **2.1 Direct or proxy; construct validity**: Is it a direct or proxy measure of the construct in 1.2? If it is a proxy, what gap between the number and the construct could mislead a user of this metric, and what evidence says the gap is small? Keep this short when the metric obviously is the thing (validity_concern: low); say more when it is contested (moderate or high). The research method and route in the front matter link to strategy pages that hold the caveats shared by all metrics using that route.
  - **2.2 Reliability**: Would the metric give the same answer twice: across judges, occasions, samples? Known figures.
  - **2.3 Data collection and measurement error**: Online / offline / in-lab / field. Instruments. Sources of measurement error and statistical uncertainty.
  - **2.4 Computation**: Data selection, transformation, aggregation. The formula. Common variants (e.g. @k, macro vs micro).
  - **2.5 Unit and scale**: Unit, range, scale type (ratio / interval / ordinal), what "good" looks like, benchmarks.
- **3. Why it is measured**: In which situations, and for which purposes, do people pull up this number?
  - **3.1 Use cases**: 2-4 concrete scenarios where this metric would actually be pulled up, real or realistic. Name a specific system, team, or decision, not a generic task category (e.g. not just "information retrieval") -- these are seeds for a reviewer to swap in their own. Different fields' uses of the same computation (e.g. precision in search vs in classification) go here.
  - **3.2 Purposes**: Evaluation, documentation, comparison, improvement, monitoring... and for which kinds of systems.
  - **3.3 Assumptions**: Hidden beliefs. What reference anchors the unit? For system metrics: the assumed user model. For user metrics: the assumed user population.
- **4. Who is involved**: Who collects the number, who acts on it, and what can go wrong once people act on it?
  - **4.1 Measurers and cost of measuring**: Who collects it, what it takes (participants, equipment, expertise, time).
  - **4.2 Consumers and decisions informed**: Who reads it; which decisions it informs.
  - **4.3 Risks: misuse, misinterpretation, gaming, Goodhart's law**: Is it prone to misuse or misreading? Can it be gamed, and who has the incentive? This is the consequential side of validity: what happens to the link between number and construct once people start optimizing the number or are judged by it.
- **5. Related metrics**: Which other metrics share its computation, its construct, or its movements?
  - **5.1 Operationally related metrics**: Metrics that share computation with this one: variants, parts, metrics built from it or derived from it (e.g. F1 and average precision from precision; NASA-TLX subscales). Front matter kind: operational.
  - **5.2 Conceptually related metrics**: Metrics that measure the same or a related construct, including siblings in the construct DAG (precision and recall). Say whether each is a substitute or a complement, and the trade-off between cost and directness. Front matter kind: conceptual. Metrics that share a construct or a neighbouring construct are listed automatically below.
  - **5.3 Empirical co-movements**: If this metric moves, how do others move? Known correlations (with sign and strength), trade-offs and causal links, with evidence. Front matter kind: empirical.
- **6. Links to business objectives**: Sustained use over time; improved usability. State direction, evidence strength, and the causal path assumed. Be explicit when the link is only theoretical.
- **7. Sources**: Rendered list of the keys in front matter `sources`, with what each one supports.
- **Open questions and review notes**: Unresolved issues, disagreements, things to verify.

## Construct pages

Source: [`schema/construct-template.md`](construct-template.md)

### Front matter fields

- `id`: lowercase-hyphenated; must equal the file name without .md
- `title`: may carry a field prefix, e.g. "Retrieval effectiveness: topical relevance"
- `aliases`: "concept" is a plain-language synonym for construct
- `broader`: parent construct ids; more than one allowed. Edit here or in constructs/dag.yaml, then run scripts/sync_constructs.py

### Sections

- **1. Definition**: What is this construct, in plain language? What would it mean for a system or a person to have more or less of it?
- **2. Boundaries**: How does it differ from its broader construct(s) and from its siblings? What is it often confused with?
- **3. Narrower constructs**: How do the narrower constructs relate to each other: do they add up to this one, trade off, or cover different situations? The list itself is generated from their `broader` fields.
- **4. Metrics**: Which metrics measure this construct, and when would you choose one over another? What part of the construct does no current metric capture? The list is generated from metric pages' construct.measures.
- **5. Links to business objectives**: Theoretical paths from this construct to sustained use and improved usability (e.g. perceived usefulness -> intention to use). Measured evidence belongs on metric pages.
- **6. How it is identified**: Where does this construct come from: theory, prior literature, or qualitative work such as interviews, diary studies, and observation that showed it matters to users?
- **7. Sources**: Rendered list of the keys in front matter `sources`, with what each one supports.
- **Open questions and review notes**: Placement in the DAG, naming, disagreements, things to verify.

## Strategy pages

Source: [`schema/strategy-template.md`](strategy-template.md)

### Front matter fields

- `id`: lowercase-hyphenated; must equal the file name without .md
- `layer`: route | method
- `research_methods`: route pages only: research_method values this route typically works on; empty on method pages

### Sections

- **1. What it is**: Route: how does it produce a number, and from what kind of data? Method: how does it gather data, and what does the data record?
  - **1.1 Definition**: Plain-language description of the route or method.
  - **1.2 Typical pairings**
  - **1.3 Named practices**: Well-known forms, each in a sentence or two (e.g. Cranfield test collections, LLM-as-a-judge, interleaving). A practice that combines a method and a route goes on both pages, briefly on one of them.
- **2. When to use it**: What measurement needs does it fit, and when is another route or method better?
- **3. Shared caveats**: Caveats that hold for every metric using this route or method, so metric pages need not repeat them. Method pages keep to what comes from gathering the data (who and what is sampled, what is recorded); caveats about scoring belong on route pages.
  - **3.1 Validity: how the number can miss the construct**: Route: typical gaps between the number and what people want to know. Method: what the data cannot show.
  - **3.2 Reliability**: Typical sources of inconsistency (judges, occasions, samples) and how they are checked.
  - **3.3 Bias and gaming**: Systematic biases of the route; how it can be gamed.
- **4. Cost**: What it takes: people, equipment, reference data, compute; what is reusable once built.
- **5. Metrics using this strategy**: Generated from metric pages' operationalization.route (route pages) or operationalization.research_method (method pages).
- **6. Sources**: Rendered list of the keys in front matter `sources`, with what each one supports.
- **Open questions and review notes**: Unresolved issues, disagreements, things to verify.
